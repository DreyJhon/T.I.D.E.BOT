T.I.D.E.BOT (Trash Inspection and Debris Extraction Bot) is an Arduino-based semi-autonomous surface vehicle being developed to collect floating solid waste near the shoreline of Pachoca, Calapan City, Oriental Mindoro.

Project overview

Engineering a practical response to floating waste

This research focuses on the design, development, and performance evaluation of a small surface vehicle intended to complement shoreline cleanup activities. It combines accessible robotics components with principles from physics and biology.

🌊

Research Locale

Pachoca, Calapan City, Oriental Mindoro, where the study will examine the collection of floating solid waste near the shoreline.

🛠️

Design Approach

A product-development and experimental evaluation approach will be used to build, test, and assess the prototype under controlled and field conditions.

♻️

Intended Contribution

A student-built, affordable technology concept that may support community cleanup efforts. It is designed to complement, not replace, existing waste-management programs.

The problem

Floating waste does not stop at the shoreline

Plastic bottles, sachets, food packaging, and wrappers can accumulate in coastal waters. These materials can affect aquatic organisms and the condition of local waterways. T.I.D.E.BOT explores whether a small, semi-autonomous vehicle can help collect suitable floating solid waste in a defined test area.

Research focus: Can the prototype collect floating solid waste while maintaining stable flotation, usable propulsion, obstacle detection, and safe operation?
Primary goal

Design, develop, and evaluate T.I.D.E.BOT using accessible materials for floating-waste collection near Pachoca's shoreline.

Scope

Small-scale collection of suitable floating solid waste in the selected coastal test area. Microplastics, dissolved pollutants, and water-quality treatment are outside the stated scope.

The prototype

How T.I.D.E.BOT is designed to work

The Arduino Uno processes distance readings from two ultrasonic sensors and controls the propeller-driven motors. As the vehicle moves, floating waste is guided into an integrated collection net.

🧠

Arduino Uno R3

The main controller. It reads sensor signals and runs the programmed movement logic.

📡

Dual HC-SR04 Sensors

Two ultrasonic sensors provide distance measurements for detecting nearby obstacles. Performance must be tested in the actual operating environment.

⚙️

L298N Motor Driver

Interfaces the controller with two DC geared motors, which rotate the rear propellers to produce thrust and help steer the vehicle.

🛟

Floating Hull

A lightweight structure, planned around floating materials, must support the electronics and the weight of collected waste while remaining stable.

🧺

Collection Net

An integrated nylon-mesh net is intended to retain suitable floating solid waste for later removal and proper disposal.

🔋

Power and Protection

Rechargeable batteries supply power. A waterproof enclosure and insulated connections are needed to protect electronics from water exposure.

Research methodology

From concept to field evaluation

The research plan follows a product-development process. The website describes the planned procedure, not completed results.

Needs assessment

Observe the selected shoreline and document suitable floating waste and relevant environmental conditions.

Construction

Assemble the floating structure, collection net, electronics, sensors, motor driver, and propulsion system.

Pre-testing

Check flotation, waterproofing, movement, sensor response, and basic operation in a controlled test before field use.

Evaluation

Conduct approved trials, record observations consistently, and analyze the measured performance.

Performance evaluation

What the team plans to measure

The final evaluation should report actual trial data. No collection-efficiency figures or validated performance claims are presented here because verified T.I.D.E.BOT results have not been supplied for this page.

Waste collection

Record the amount or count of waste collected per trial, using the same starting conditions and suitable waste types where possible.

Obstacle detection

Record sensor readings and whether the robot responds as intended when obstacles are encountered.

Navigation and runtime

Observe movement and steering, battery operating time, and any interruptions during each trial.

Mechanical frame

Inspect structural stability and the ability to support the device and collected load.

Waterproofing

Check the enclosure and connections for signs of water ingress before and after testing.

Expert evaluation

Use the research team's approved checklist to document functionality, safety, reliability, and suitability.
